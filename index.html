The order you eat your food in changes your blood sugar.

Not what you eat. Not how much. The order. Eat the vegetables and the chicken before you touch the rice, and the spike that follows is around a third smaller than if you had started with the rice — same meal, same grams, same calories.

        Where the numbers came from, and what they can't tell you

        Read this before you trust the curve

        • This is a teaching model of a healthy adult, not a clinical simulator and not medical advice. It has no idea who you are.
        • Real responses to the same meal in the same person vary from day to day, and vary far more between people. Two people eating this plate would get two different curves; a continuous glucose monitor is the only way to see yours.
        • The order effect here is calibrated to a study in prediabetes — about a third off the peak. The original pilot in type 2 diabetes found a much bigger effect, 73% off the area under the curve, and this page deliberately does not use that headline number, because you probably are not that group.
        • Glycaemic index is a population average with a wide spread. White rice ranges from 48 to 92 depending on the cultivar. Treat every GI number here as "about".
        • The weight panel uses a linear 7,700 kcal per kilogram. That is reasonable for one week and increasingly wrong after that, because what you burn falls as you lose mass. It projects one week for exactly that reason.
